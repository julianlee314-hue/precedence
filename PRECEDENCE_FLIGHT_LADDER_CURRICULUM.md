# Precedence — Flight ladder (Eras 11–20) curriculum draft

*Math Sensei · 3 Oct 2026 · **implemented on Pages** (Hangar wing; 11–15 full banks, 16–20 light stubs)*

**Premise.** Same game loop as Precedence: the player never does the arithmetic. They only tap **what comes next** in a brutally nested expression. A calculator (or the collapse engine) does the “how much.” This ladder trains the *other* brain region — the one that fails on hard precalc and first-year calc exams: **seeing structure under pressure.**

**Band.** Eras **11–20** after the ground/road ten (Sums → Fraction bars). Miami Burnout (Era 8) stays the road **1.0**. Flight is the **1.5 / advanced** wing — unlocked after Fraction bars (or after a Road Test on Era 10).

**UI titles stay math.** Flight craft are **visual-only** (same lock as Cleopatra litter). Garage cards read the math titles below; the hangar silhouette tips the fantasy.

**Nomenclature (unchanged).** Era → Levels (stages) → Run → Step. Algebra Licenses remain a separate wing.

## 2. Curriculum map — math titles + skills

### A. Precalc order-mastery (Eras 11–15)

| Era | Math title | What “next” means | Classic exam fails it trains | Emphasized skills (Mathera-ish) |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **11** | **Grouping towers** | Innermost grouping first across `( )`, `| |`, `√`, and nested radicals | Treating √(a+b) like √a+√b; abs vs parens; “outside first” | Nested grouping; absolute value as grouping; radical as unary wrapper — extend `III.1.08` / precalc grouping IDs |
| **12** | **Fraction architecture** | Which bar / vinculum binds first; complex fractions as towers | Cancelling across addition; attacking the wrong floor of a complex fraction | Complex fractions; rational nesting; extend Era 10 fraction-bar line |
| **13** | **Composition order** | Read `f(g(h(x)))` and `(f∘g)(x)` left/right correctly; inverse as undo order | Computing g(f(x)) when asked f(g(x)); inverse steps backwards | Function composition; inverses as reverse order — new composition skill IDs |
| **14** | **Trig reading** | `sin²x` vs `sin(x²)` vs `sin⁻¹`; degree/radian is *not* the point — **power vs composition** is | Squaring the wrong thing; inverse trig vs reciprocal | Trig notation literacy (power, composition, inverse) — testing-mode trig grain |
| **15** | **Exp–log stacks** | Log of a power vs power of a log; change-of-base as regrouping | Moving exponents through logs illegally; expanding too early | Exp/log laws as *order* moves; change of base as rewrite sequence |

Hard-precalc exam spirit: IB AA HL / AP Precalc / UK A-level pure “simplify / rearrange without evaluating” items.

### B. Calculus class (Eras 16–20)

User ask: limits, derivatives, integrals + **two more**. Proposal for the pair: **infinite series structure** and **multivariable / implicit chain** (the two places order errors stay lethal after Calc I).

| Era | Math title | What “next” means | Classic fail | Why this pair fits |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **16** | **Limits structure** | Algebraic legal next step *inside* a limit (factor → cancel → plug; conjugate; dominant term) before “evaluate” | Plugging first; canceling terms that aren’t factors | Glass cockpit: read instruments before trusting the number |
| **17** | **Derivative protocol** | Which differentiation rule applies *next* (const, sum, product, quotient, chain) — pick the rule, don’t compute the derivative by hand | Chain last when it should be first; quotient vs product misread | Afterburner: one wrong rule order = flameout |
| **18** | **Integral protocol** | Recognition order: rewrite → choose technique (u-sub vs parts vs partial fractions) → execute skeleton | Parts when u-sub; differential in the wrong place | Stealth: the technique is half-hidden until you see structure |
| **19** | **Series structure** | Term-by-term legality; geometric vs Taylor skeleton; what you may differentiate/integrate termwise | Rearranging divergent series; wrong center; factorial vs exponential growth misread | Drone swarm: many terms, one legal move |
| **20** | **Several variables** | Partial vs total; which variable is held fixed; implicit / related-rates dependency order; mixed partials equality as a *check*, not a first move | Treating ∂ as d; chain rule missing a path | Elite hangar: no single “forward” — you choose the axis |

**Alternate for Era 19–20** if you want to stay single-variable longer: **19 Related rates / implicit**, **20 Diff EQ / separation order**. I’d still prefer series + multivariable for “hard exam that still isn’t about punching the calculator.”

## 3. Stages within each Era

**Recommendation: 6 stages per Era** (plus an optional 7th mixed road test).

| Stage # | Role | Purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Lexicon** | One new notation habit in isolation (e.g. only `sin²` vs `sin( )`) |
| 2 | **Trap set A** | Classic wrong-first moves as distractor cards / wrong taps |
| 3 | **Trap set B** | Second failure mode (different shape, same skill) |
| 4 | **Combine** | New habit + previous Era’s habit in one expression |
| 5 | **Dense** | Exam-length nests; still no arithmetic grind |
| 6 | **Fluency** | Timed Rally / Forks; streak = altitude |
| 7 (opt.) | **Road test / Checkride** | 12 mixed; 1 slip OK → stamp Era license / unlock next craft |

**Why 6 not 10–12:** Algebra Licenses already run ~10–15 levels deep for *rewrite* pedagogy. Flight eras are **protocol trainers** — fewer stages, nastier trees, more repetition via generator tiers (0–2) rather than more named stages. 6×10 = **60 stages** for the whole flight wing; with tiers that’s plenty of runway without feeling like a second ALEKS.

**Stamps:** keep Practice-can-clear (e.g. 8 clean → stamp, 4 stamps → Era cleared) — slightly tighter than road eras because each stage is denser.

## 4. What stays out of scope (on purpose)

- Numeric evaluation, CAS grinding, GDC button sequences (that’s Testing Mode / Notebooks).
- Full proof writing / FRQ paragraphs (overlay later).
- Teaching *new* calculus ideas from zero — Flight assumes the idea exists; it trains **order under complexity**.
- Replacing Algebra Licenses — those stay the rewrite/equivalence wing.
